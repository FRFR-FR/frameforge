# Episode brief — EP002

## The Machine That Prints the World's Most Advanced Chips

**Central question:** How can a factory machine turn a stream of molten tin droplets into extreme-ultraviolet light, then use mirrors and precision motion to print patterns onto silicon?

**Viewer promise:** Follow one exposure from the light source through the reflective optical path, reticle and wafer stage. Understand why 13.5 nm matters, why air and ordinary glass are a problem, and what High-NA EUV has begun to change.

**Editorial angle:** Explain one real machine without treating it as a magical chip printer. EUV is one lithography tool in a much larger process involving deposition, etch, cleaning, inspection, metrology and process control.

**Narrative progression:** cold open; chip layers; DUV versus EUV wavelength; tin-droplet plasma; mirrors and vacuum; reticle and photoresist; synchronized wafer stage; repeated alignment; 2026 High-NA milestone; manufacturing ecosystem payoff.

**Visual approach:** 75–85% real, relevant, moving semiconductor and precision-manufacturing footage. Use ten dynamic 12-second 3D cutaways only where the camera cannot show the invisible mechanism. Generic industrial footage is context, never evidence of a named factory or proprietary process.

**On-screen policy:** one small channel bug; brief chapter labels over moving footage; readable subtitles generated from actual Kokoro sentence timings; restrained navy/blue subtitle backplates, white type, red chapter accents and a thin red top rule. No static slide sequence, title-card presentation or text-heavy panels.

**Brand palette:** red #D62839, white #FFFFFF, blue #1746A2, deep navy #102B52. Preserve footage's natural colours.

**Factual guardrails:** attribute vendor specifications; do not describe chip node names as literal measurements of all transistor features; do not imply every chip layer uses EUV; scope High-NA HVM claims to selected layers on a subset of products; do not describe the million-wafer aggregate as one million finished chips.

**Release gate:** at least 70% relevant moving B-roll (75–85% target), ten conceptual cutaways correctly identified, rights manifest complete, every selected clip visually reviewed, narration WAV/MP3 from Kokoro Echo, subtitle timing checked, 1280×720 H.264/AAC, measured fps reported, full decode and freeze checks passed, representative frames inspected, and human approval before publication.
