# EP001 — The Secret Cables That Keep the Internet Alive

## Episode brief

**Working title:** The Secret Cables That Keep the Internet Alive  
**Alternative titles:**
1. The Hidden Cables Carrying the World's Internet
2. Almost All Global Internet Traffic Travels Through These Cables
3. The Underwater Network Connecting the World

Use title 2 only if the script cites a current authoritative source and carefully explains the metric. Avoid implying every internet connection travels through a submarine cable at every stage.

**Format:** 8–10 minute cinematic documentary; delivered pilot is 8:54.667 at a measured 18 fps  
**Master:** 1280×720 (720p), 16:9, H.264 MP4 + AAC; target 24–30 fps only when measured output supports it  
**Audience promise:** By the end, viewers understand how submarine fibre-optic cables move data between continents, how they are installed and repaired, and why this hidden infrastructure matters.

## Core narrative

The internet feels wireless, but international connectivity depends heavily on physical infrastructure on the ocean floor. The story follows a message from one continent to another, reveals the cable system beneath the sea, then explains how engineers install, protect and repair it.

### Preferred source families

- International Telecommunication Union (ITU)
- International Cable Protection Committee (ICPC)

- National telecommunications regulators
- Peer-reviewed optical communications research

Record exact source URLs, publication dates, access dates and the specific claims supported. Treat commercial maps and graphics as copyrighted unless their terms permit reuse. Create an original simplified map if permission is unclear.

## Opening narration draft

"Every second, people send messages, watch videos and move information between continents. It feels as if the internet is everywhere and nowhere at once. But beneath the surface of the oceans, a vast physical network helps make that connection possible: fibre-optic cables laid across the seabed. And when one of those cables fails, the problem is not just underwater. It can affect the routes that connect people, businesses and entire regions. So how does this hidden network actually work?"

## Visual asset plan

Priority order:
1. Licensed real footage of cable-laying ships and repair vessels.
2. Officially released imagery from cable operators, research organizations and government sources, subject to reuse terms.
3. Original 3D cable cross-section and fibre-optic explanation.
4. Original animated map using a dataset whose license permits the intended use.
5. Licensed ocean, ship and data-centre footage for transitions.

Avoid:
- Unlicensed footage from other documentaries.
- Generic AI ocean imagery presented as real footage.
- Maps that imply exact routes when the data is generalized.
- Unverified claims about cable tapping, sabotage or precise vulnerabilities.
- Reusing one stock clip repeatedly to fill runtime.

## Shorts concepts

1. **"The internet has a physical floor"** — 25–35 seconds explaining that international connectivity relies on seabed cables.
2. **"What happens when an underwater internet cable breaks?"** — 30–45 seconds explaining rerouting and repair at a high level.
3. **"Why is this cable armoured?"** — 20–30 seconds comparing shallow-water protection with deep-sea cable construction.

Each Short needs its own opening, context and payoff. Do not simply crop a random section from the long video.
