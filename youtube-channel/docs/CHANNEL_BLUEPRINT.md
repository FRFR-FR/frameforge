# Underneath Everything — Channel Blueprint

## 1. Positioning

**Working channel name:** Underneath Everything  
**Editorial promise:** Cinematic explanations of the hidden infrastructure, machines and engineering behind everyday life.  
**Audience:** Curious English-speaking viewers, initially targeting broad international reach in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and other English-speaking markets. Geography is an audience hypothesis, not a guarantee of distribution or RPM.

The channel is not a generic AI-news feed. It is a documentary brand with recurring themes, visual consistency and a clear reason to watch the next episode.

## 2. Content pillars and launch mix

| Pillar | Initial share | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Hidden infrastructure | 40% | Submarine cables, power grids, ports, water systems, data centres |
| Extraordinary engineering | 25% | Tunnel boring machines, bridges, megaprojects, heavy machinery |
| Technology explained | 20% | Chips, GPS, internet routing, satellites, AI infrastructure |
| Future systems | 10% | Robotics, energy storage, autonomous infrastructure |
| Forgotten inventions | 5% | Obsolete systems, overlooked breakthroughs, failed designs |

Keep the first 8–12 uploads concentrated around infrastructure and engineering. Expand only when analytics show audience overlap.

## 3. Format and visual identity

- Long-form master: 1920×1080, 30 fps, progressive, H.264 MP4, AAC stereo.
- Runtime target: 8–12 minutes; let the story determine the final duration.
- Shorts: 1080×1920, 30 fps, 20–45 seconds.
- Narration: clear, conversational, expressive; avoid synthetic over-emphasis.
- Music: properly licensed, mixed below narration, with no distracting constant effects.
- Captions: accurate, synchronized, readable on mobile.
- Visual language: real documentary footage, restrained cinematic grading, custom maps, technical diagrams and selective 3D cutaways.
- No blur, vignette, fake film damage, random overlays or decorative effects without a storytelling purpose.
- Avoid generic AI-generated imagery when a real source image, archival clip, map or accurate 3D explanation is available.

## 4. Story structure

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:20):** A striking fact, consequence or visual question. Do not begin with a logo or lengthy greeting.
2. **Promise (0:20–0:50):** Explain the central question and why it matters.
3. **Context (0:50–2:00):** Establish the scale and the problem.
4. **Mechanism (2:00–6:30):** Explain how the system works with visual evidence.
5. **Consequences and tension (6:30–9:30):** Show what can go wrong, what engineers do about it, and what is changing.
6. **Payoff (final 30–60 sec):** Resolve the opening question and lead naturally to a related subject.

Use this as a pacing guide, not a rigid template. Avoid padding.

## 5. Packaging and growth

For every episode, prepare:
- Three title candidates with distinct angles.
- Two genuinely different thumbnail compositions.
- A one-sentence viewer promise.
- A description with sources and relevant context.
- Chapters only after final runtime is known.
- Two or three Shorts concepts with separate hooks.

Thumbnail rules:
- One primary subject, one clear visual idea, strong contrast.
- Keep text to 0–4 short words, or omit it.
- No misleading scale, fake disasters, invented damage or false claims.
- Check legibility at phone size.

Growth loop:
- Review impressions, click-through rate, first-30-second retention, average view duration, returning viewers and subscribers per 1,000 views.
- Compare videos against the channel's own baseline; do not treat generic benchmark numbers as guarantees.
- If impressions are low, revisit topic demand and packaging.
- If clicks are healthy but early retention is weak, improve the opening and promise-delivery match.
- If retention is strong but reach is low, test adjacent topics and packaging before abandoning the subject.
- Make follow-ups when a topic produces returning viewers or strong satisfaction signals.

## 6. Publishing cadence

First 30 days:
- Target 6–8 long-form episodes, not 12 if quality would suffer.
- Target 10–16 Shorts derived from the same research, but edited as standalone stories.
- Publish consistently and preserve time for review and iteration.
- Increase to 2–3 long-form uploads weekly only after the team can sustain research, rights clearance, visual quality and review.

## 7. Monetization and policy guardrails

- Full YouTube ad-revenue eligibility generally requires 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the preceding 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days; check YouTube's current official requirements before applying.
- Shorts-feed watch time does not count toward the 4,000-hour long-form threshold.
- Do not rely on repetitive, templated, mass-produced or minimally transformed content. Each episode needs original research, a distinct narrative, editorial judgment and substantive explanation.
- Keep records for every footage, music, image and data asset: source URL, creator, license, download date and any attribution requirement.
- Do not use unlicensed movie/TV clips, other creators' edits or unverified social-media footage.
- Clearly disclose realistic synthetic or altered content when YouTube's rules require it.
- If the channel owner is under 18, a parent or guardian must handle the AdSense account as required by Google's rules.

## 8. South Africa operating notes

- Produce in English and use globally understandable units; include metric units first where useful.
- Schedule uploads based on audience analytics once enough data exists, not assumptions about US prime time.
- Keep source/license records and production costs in a simple spreadsheet.
- Avoid buying expensive AI subscriptions before the format proves audience demand.
- Test the workflow on a short pilot before committing to a full production batch.

## 9. Definition of done

An episode is publish-ready only when:
- All factual claims have traceable sources.
- Names, dates, numbers and technical descriptions have been checked.
- Every asset has a documented usage basis.
- Narration is intelligible and synchronized.
- No black frames, missing media, broken captions or accidental silence remain.
- The thumbnail and title accurately represent the episode.
- The final MP4 has been watched and listened to in full or checked with a documented QC process.
