One message. One click. One stolen account. Phishing uses fake emails, texts, or alerts that look familiar and push you to act before you think. The goal is to steal information or send you to an unsafe page.

Check the sender's full address: a display name can be copied, but the real domain may look wrong. Watch for pressure such as urgent, suspended, or act now. Be careful with unexpected links and attachments, especially when the destination does not match the organisation. Treat any request for passwords, payment details, or one-time sign-in codes as a major warning. Never share a verification code in a message.

Do not click the button. Open the official app or type the known website address yourself. Look for the alert there. Need help? Contact the organisation through a number or channel you already trust—not the details inside the message. Report the message as phishing, then delete it.

Already clicked? Do not enter any details. Close the page and open the real service. Change any password you exposed, turn on multi-factor authentication, and quickly contact your bank or workplace IT team if payment or work accounts may be involved.
